# The design audit, 2026-08-29

What was audited, what it found, and what it decided. Every number here came from a bench in
this repo that can be re-run for free; the bench name is given so nothing has to be taken on
trust.

The organising idea: `bench:unchecked` corrupts every value that places copper and asks whether
anything objects. It found two holes on its first run. **The symbol, the 3D solid and the two
emitters had never had an equivalent.** So the first job was symmetry of coverage.

---

## A. The three areas that had no instrument

### A1. The symbol — `bench:symbol`

Two halves. The first forces each defect an emitter could plausibly introduce and asks whether
the symbol's own check reports it. The second corrupts a value on the RECORD and asks whether
anything in the product objects.

**Before:** 2 of 6 emitter defects went unreported on one part; a swapped pair of pin names left
the product's verdict completely unchanged on 43 of 89 parts.

**After:** 6 of 6 reported on all 107 parts; swapped names caught on every part.

Two defects, both fixed:

**1. `symbolViolations` returned early on grid-addressed parts.** The branch that checks a BGA's
terminals returned before the name check and the shared-anchor check, so renaming one of
LP5907's DSBGA pins on the way out, or drawing two of them on one point, was reported on all 106
other parts and not on that one. Both checks now run on every part; only *which numbers the
symbol must carry* is grid-specific.

**2. `pinevidence.ts` confirmed a corrupted pinout, 46 times out of 107.** This is the serious
one, because pin evidence is THE second source for the netlist under the invariant.

A sequence of collinear pin numbers picks the offset its claimed names sit at, and confirms the
pins that sit at it. With only ONE pin supporting the offset that is circular: the offset is
derived from the single match it then goes on to confirm. ADG1211's page 7 throws off two stray
pairs of numbers, `[2,16]` and `[1,15]`; each picked an offset two hundred points across the page,
each agreed with itself, and the page-level union used that to **erase the dissent the page's real
pin table had just recorded**. Swap two of that part's pin names and the product still called the
pinout confirmed.

`bench:pinsweep` scores both floors against both objectives at once, which the 2026-08-27 sweep
could not: an oracle can only tell you that a reading you got RIGHT was confirmed, never what the
check would do with one you got wrong.

```
 seq  support  KEEPS  oracle ok  FALSE  SWAPPED  SHIFTED
   2        1     71         23      0       46       30     <- what shipped
   2        2     62         21      0        3        7
   2        3     58         21      0        1        3     <- now
   2        4     55         21      0        1        2
   3        3     58         21      0        1        3
```

`MIN_OFFSET_SUPPORT` is 3. It costs 13 corroborated pinouts, two of them oracle-verified, and
takes corrupted pinouts that still come back confirmed from 46 to 1 and shifted ones from 30 to 3.

**A fix that read well and bought nothing, reverted.** The first diagnosis was the page-level
union: AD8221's connection diagram and its pin table BOTH decline to agree about pin 1 after a
swap, and a third page supplies it. Restricting confirmation to a single page fixes that part.
Measured across the corpus it costs seven corroborated pinouts, three oracle-verified, and
changes the corrupted-pinout count by **zero** once the self-confirming offset is closed. It was
reverted on the measurement rather than kept on the argument.

**Cost of both, in the product's own terms** (`bench:confirm`): pinout confirmed 72% -> 59%,
oracle-verified confirmations 23 -> 20, false confirmations 0 before and after. That is the price
of a second source that could previously be talked into agreeing with almost anything.

### A2. The 3D solid — `bench:emitters`

The emitted STEP is parsed back and its eight corner points compared to the record.

**84 solids built, 2 refused for want of a body dimension, 0 disagreements.** Every one is the
record's own body, length, width and height, seated on the board plane at z=0. The July defects
(a guessed body from the pin count, a box straddling the board plane) are genuinely gone.

### A3. Do the two emitters agree? — `bench:emitters`

`ARCHITECTURE.md` promises KiCad and Altium are peers, neither derived from the other. Nothing had
ever checked it: each was tested against the geometry it was handed, in its own file, on its own
hand-built fixture.

Both formats are now built for every part the cache can rebuild and read back by readers that did
not write them — kiutils for KiCad, AltiumSharp for Altium — and compared **to each other**.

**86 parts. Every numbered land matches in position and size, every paste aperture matches, every
symbol pin matches in number, name and position.**

Three instrument bugs had to be fixed before that sentence meant anything, and all three would
have read as product defects:

- paste apertures carry no designator in either format, so keying on the empty string collapsed
  fourteen of them onto one entry and reported two hundred disagreements between a pad and an
  unrelated aperture
- Altium stores the pin end that touches the BODY and KiCad stores the far end, so comparing the
  two stored numbers reported all 1623 pins as disagreeing by exactly one stub
- Altium stores coordinates on an integer 1/10000 mil grid, so a land at 0.9125 mm comes back as
  0.913 and a string comparison called three correct footprints wrong

The bench now **proves itself before it reports**: on the first part it moves a land, renames a
pin and inflates the solid, and refuses to print anything if all three are not reported.

## B. The open design questions, settled by counting

### B4 + B5. Where do power pins go? — `bench:powerpins`, `bench:pintypes`

Counted over the official KiCad symbol library, 22,871 symbols:

| | op-amps (208) | MCUs (2841) |
|---|---|---|
| power pins on the top/bottom edges | **99.5%** | **99.8%** |
| uses all four edges | 100% | 91.7% |
| multi-unit | 33.7% | 7.4% |

Our generator puts every pin on the left or the right. The published convention, and KLC S4.3,
say power belongs on the rails. So the default looks wrong.

**It is not changed, and the reason is a second count.** Placing power pins on the rails needs
every power pin identified, and a symbol where SOME supply pins are on the rail and others
identical to them are on the side is worse than one uniform layout — the difference between the
two would mean nothing.

- the document states an electrical type for **every** pin on **6 of 107** parts
- 15 more state it for some pins (`ADS8688: 1 of 38`, `PCM1808: 9 of 14`)
- a deliberately conservative name rule (`VCC|VDD|VSS|GND|V+|V-|...` and nothing that merely
  carries a voltage) scores **100% precision and 44% recall** against the pins whose type the
  document does state: 44 right, **0 wrong**, 56 missed

Zero false positives is a good result and 44% recall is fatal to the change: on AD7606 it finds
`AVCC` and leaves `V1GND` through `V7GND` on the side. We cannot know we have found them all, so
we do not move any.

**Recorded as a deliberate decision, not an oversight.** It reverses if extraction ever states
types for whole parts rather than fragments — the trigger is `bench:pintypes` reporting most parts
in the "every pin typed" row rather than 6.

### The electrical type is not vouched for, by decision

`bench:symbol` reports that setting every pin on every part to `power` changes nothing any check,
gate or confirmation says. That stands, and it is a position rather than a hole. RULES.md: "a
pairing that cannot name two different means is not a confirmation, say so rather than inventing
one." The only candidate second source is the pin name, and reading a type out of a name is the
invention this project exists not to do — `EN` is an input on one part and an open-drain output on
the next. The type is a READ value with a citation or it is `unspecified`; a wrong one produces no
wrong copper and no wrong net. If it ever starts driving something that reaches a board, that
stops being defensible.

**One real defect found on the way, fixed.** The two extraction passes read the same pin table and
only pass 1 tends to reach the type column; pass 2 answers `null` and then WINS, discarding what
pass 1 read. 878 cached answers carry electrical types and 90 of 107 parts were reaching the
symbol with every pin `unspecified`. `combine` now fills a missing type from the other pass where
the pin number AND its name agree, so nothing is carried across from a different pinout. It fills
gaps only and can never overwrite an answer.

**And one instrument that could not see the field at all.** `replayRecords` hardcoded
`electricalType: "unspecified"`, so `bench:pintypes` first reported 3114 of 3114 pins untyped and
every bench built on that harness — `bench:unchecked` included — was blind to the field.

### B6. `.IntLib` or loose files?

Settled without a count: an `.IntLib` is the **compile output** of Altium Designer's own compiler
over a `.LibPkg` containing exactly a `.SchLib` and a `.PcbLib`. It is not a format a generator can
emit. Shipping the two source libraries is both what we can do and what a user needs in order to
compile one themselves. Our output shape is right.

### B7. The span cross-check's false-positive rate

Not measurable: the check it refers to does not exist in the code. It collapses into item C, the
paid question of whether the model can read dimension-line endpoints, and that decision should be
taken against the size of the problem rather than in the abstract — `bench:copper` currently
reports **no disagreement between the copper and the record it was built from** on 80 footprints.

## The two holes `bench:unchecked` was already reporting

Both were open findings before today. Both are closed.

### `leadSides` had no second source at all — 59 silent

Swap 2 sides for 4 and the lands go from two rows to four edges: a footprint the
part cannot be placed on. `bench:unchecked` swapped it on 86 footprints the
product vouched for and **59 stayed CONFIRMED**, because every other input is
individually fine and nothing read the side count off anything but the drawing it
was counted on.

Two candidate second sources, both scored before either was built
(`bench:sidesweep`):

| | covers | agrees | catches a swap |
|---|---|---|---|
| the leads have to fit along the body edge | 98 parts | — | 25 of 98, 0 correct parts flagged at a bound of 1.1 |
| **the package family name** | **98 of 103** | **97, one disagreement** | every swap |

The Q in QFP is quad; SOIC, SSOP, TSOT, DFN, SON and a ceramic flat pack are two
rows; a TO can is one; a BGA and an LGA have no sides and `declaredLeadSides`
answers null rather than guessing. The product already reads a lead COUNT out of
the same string, so this is the same liberty and no new one.

The name won and the geometry rule was **not built**: one source covering 95% of
the corpus is worth more than a second covering a quarter of it, and RULES.md is
explicit that a bound which rarely fires is decorating a check rather than
carrying it.

**Result: 76 confirmed, 76 caught, 0 silent.** It costs 2 flags on the whole
corpus, both LGA parts where the honest answer is that no family name states a
side count.

The one disagreement it reports is `TPS61022 "VQFN-HR (7)"` — a seven-pin
half-etched QFN read as two-sided. A flag is the right outcome there.

### `confirmBody` was a one-sided bound — 56 silent

The body was paired against the lead span that has to reach past it. Two callouts
on ONE drawing, which is not two means, and one-sided besides: shrink a body and
its own span still reaches past it. `bench:unchecked` shrank every body to 40% of
its real size and **56 stayed CONFIRMED**.

Two bounds were swept (`bench:bodysweep`) and the first was **rejected**:

- **span / body**, across 85 parts, runs 1.02 to 3.86. A bound tight enough to
  catch anything flags LMP7704-SP, a correct reading of a rad-hard ceramic flat
  pack whose unformed leads genuinely reach 3.86 times across its body. No single
  ratio both fires and is safe.
- **the printed footprint's outer land extent / body**, across 62 parts, runs
  0.82 to 2.97 — and it is two DIFFERENT drawings, which is what the invariant
  means by a second means. A bound of 3.5 flags **none** of the correct parts,
  clears the widest by 18%, and catches 41 of 62 shrunken bodies. A bound of 3.0
  catches 47 and clears the widest correct reading by one percent, which is not
  clearance, it is luck.

**It flags and does not confirm**, for the reason the copper overlay does:
agreeing within a factor of three and a half rules out one class of error and
says nothing about the others.

**Result: 56 silent -> 20**, and it added **zero** flags on real records. The 20
that remain are parts where the datasheet prints no footprint, so there is no
second drawing to disagree with.

### A live defect found on the way: lands under the package

`spanCoversBody` exists to catch a lead span that ends inside its own body. It
reads `dimensions.leadSpanMm` — the DRAWING's span — which is null on precisely
the packages whose span comes from the settings screen instead. So on every
straight-lead ceramic flat pack it reported "unavailable" while the lands were
laid out wherever the assembler's one global number put them.

The seated span is asked **once per assembler**, because it is a property of
their forming die. One number cannot cover a 5 mm package and a 24 mm one. Two
parts in the cached corpus — RHF1201's ceramic SO48 and UT54LVDS217's 48-lead
flatpack — were shipping with every land underneath the body.

They now ask for a seated span **for that package**, and the question names both
numbers that make the install-wide one impossible. Whether a forming die gives a
constant EXTENSION beyond the body rather than a constant span is a question
about a customer's process and is not answered here; what is answered is narrower
and not in doubt, which is that leads cannot end inside the package they leave.

## C. The paid measurement, and why it was not bought

The question was whether the model can read dimension-line endpoints, which
Phase 2 of the `landSpanMm` fix assumes. It was costed against the size of the
problem first.

`bench:dimensions`, on the tuned corpus against hand-read drawings:
**CORRECT 713, WRONG 8, NOT READ 10.** The eight:

```
AD590     pitchMm         2.54            the drawing states none
ADXL345   landPadWidthMm  0.25            0.55
LTC3105   leadWidthMm     0.22-0.38       0.33-0.482
NCP1200   bodyHeightMm    1.55            1.75
RHF1201   bodyHeightMm    2.47            2.72
RHFL4913  bodyLengthMm    10.54           16.64
TLV9061   leadSpanMm      2.75-3.05       2.6-3
VA10820   leadSpanMm      15.6            13.82-14.12
```

Two span errors in 731 values, and **no `landSpanMm` errors at all**. `bench:copper`
reports no disagreement between the copper and the record it was built from on

and introduce a new failure mode. Not bought, and the money was not spent.

**Cumulative model spend, all runs ever: ~$59.57 over 2529 billed calls.** Today
added nothing: every call the hold-out needed was already cached.

## Still open, and each one needs a person

**1. Does a forming die give a constant lead extension beyond the body, or a
constant span?** The guard above refuses the impossible cases; it does not model
the right answer. If the extension is what is constant, `formedLeadSpanMm` should
be asked as an extension and added to each package's body, and the question
disappears for every flat pack at once. This is a fact about your assembly
process and no amount of counting reaches it.

**2. Open one file in Altium.** Two independent readers now agree on every pad
and every pin of 86 parts, and neither of them is Altium. That is the strongest
statement available short of opening it.

**3. Does anyone you are selling to use OrCAD?** Decides whether the Cadence
generator is worth building at all.

## Measured and deliberately not built

**Multi-unit symbols.** 33.7% of the official library's op-amp symbols and 7.4%
of its MCU symbols are split into units — a dual op-amp as two amplifiers and a
power unit. Ours are always one box. This is a real gap and a large build
(`SymbolGeometry` has no notion of a unit, and both emitters would follow), and
it needs the same thing the power-rail question needed: knowing which pins belong
to which channel, which no datasheet field we read states.

**Power pins on the top and bottom rails.** See B4/B5 above. Reverses if
`bench:pintypes` ever reports most parts in the "every pin typed" row.

**The geometric lead-side rule.** Built, measured at 25 of 98 with no false
positives, and dropped because the package name covers 95%.

# Pre-flight, before a real engineer sees it

The audit above checked whether the product is CORRECT. This checked whether a
person can use it without hitting something we could have found ourselves.

## Bad uploads — `bench:badinput`

Every instrument in this repo feeds the product a real datasheet. A person will
not. Seven things someone could plausibly upload, six of them real files:

| what | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| an empty file | refused | refused |
| a web page saved as `.pdf` | refused | refused |
| **a download that stopped halfway** | **threw, HTTP 500** | 400: "most likely an incomplete download" |
| a real PDF that is not a datasheet | 200, says what is missing | unchanged |
| **a real datasheet with no pin table** | **200, and nothing saying so** | 400-class refusal with the missing fields named |
| a file over the size limit | refused | refused |
| no file at all | refused | refused |

**Two of my own bench's first three findings were the bench.** It built its own
PDFs and pdf.js rejected both, so what it measured was a broken generator. Every
case is now a document that actually exists, and the one it cannot reach - a
scanned page with no text layer - is named as untested rather than faked.

## Three defects, all of the same shape

**1. A truncated PDF escaped as an HTTP 500.** The route caught
`PdfExtractionError`, which means "a valid document too big to parse". A file
that will not open at all threw whatever pdf.js threw, and an unrecognised throw
out of a route handler is a 500. The person saw "something went wrong" with
nothing pointing at their file. Now a typed `PdfUnreadableError` and a 400 that
says what to do.

**2. No reader configured meant HTTP 200 with a record of nulls.** The route
already guards two doors against handing back a reader-less record dressed as a
success - the budget one and the catch around the model call - and its own
comment says why. The third door, `makeExtractionModel` answering null because
no credential is set, was open. An expired key or a deployment that lost its
environment produced a 200, a page of blanks, and no hint that a reader had never
run. `bench:holdout` measures the parser alone at READ 0 of 59.

Both routes now refuse with `MODEL_UNAVAILABLE` and carry the provenance, because
the upload or the retrieval still happened and an audit trail still wants the
digest. **The parity test between the two routes is what caught the second half:**
the guard went on `/api/parse` alone and the routes immediately disagreed. That is
the third time a guard on one has had to be repeated on the other.

**3. An empty chooser reported itself as a success.** `optionsFromPerPackageTables`
returned `{ ok: true, options: [] }` whenever it found nothing, which reads
downstream as "the record is fine, here are its packages". The screen has no state
for that: the verdict card's first branch is `packageChoice.ok === false`, so an
empty success fell straight past the sentence written to explain exactly this
situation. CD4017B is the case.

## The one that would have wasted his afternoon

**A question you answer, and it asks again in the same words.**

`bench:browser --full` reports which user paths a run exercised. `question-answered`
had been in the "NOT exercised" list every run since it was written, and that was
read as "these datasheets did not ask anything". It was not. The selector was
`.ask input` and no element has the class `ask` - the markup is `.ask-group >
.ask-list > .ask-row-full > .ask-row > input`. **The stage could never fire.**

Fixing the selector, and moving the answering to where the question actually
appears (after the export refuses, not before), exercised the path for the first
time and it failed immediately:

```
[question] RHF1201.pdf: KiCad asked for 1 value(s), was answered, and still did not build
```

The generator was fine - RHF1201's ceramic SO48 has a 9.65 mm body and ships at
any span of 9.7 mm or more. The screen was the problem. `handleExport` spread the
install-wide answers AFTER the part-specific ones:

```js
...answers,   // the number the user just typed for this package
...install,   // the assembler's one global number, which overwrites it
```

So a flat pack asked for a seated span for that package, the user typed one, and
the screen sent back the very number that could not work. Same question, same
words, forever. The more specific answer now wins.

This is one of the two defects today that would have reached him, and both were
found by the same thing: driving a path nobody had driven.

# The preview

The last unblocked item on the pre-audit plan, and the one that changes what a
review session is worth.

## The problem it solves

Every other thing on the screen is a number. So the question a person was being
asked was "do you believe our arithmetic", which nobody answers by reading a
table of millimetres. Drawn, the question becomes "is that the part", which an
engineer answers in two seconds. A pin 1 in the wrong corner, a row of pads down
one side of a quad, a thermal pad the size of the package, a courtyard inside its
own copper: all obvious in a picture, all invisible in a list.

## It draws the footprint that ships, and there is no second code path

`PackageOption.geometry` is the object `createExportZip` writes into the library,
already through `validateGeometry`, carried out of the generator rather than
recomputed. **A preview built by its own code is a picture of something the user
is not going to get** - it invites them to approve one footprint and download
another. There is no geometry in the drawing component: every number is read off
the object.

Copper, paste, courtyard, body outline, thermal vias and the pin-1 marker are
drawn. The silkscreen is not: the emitters derive their own outlines, so this has
nothing true to draw and would have to invent it. An absent line is honest.

## The case that needed a second route

A part that is ASKED a question has no chooser geometry until it is answered, and
every ceramic flat pack is asked one. That is this product's own market, so the
preview would have gone missing on exactly the parts it matters most for: the
user types a formed lead span themselves and then downloads the result blind.

`/api/export` now takes `preview: true` and returns the geometry instead of the
bytes. Everything above that branch has already run - the same validation, the
same refusals, the same `createExportZip` - so a preview cannot be a different
footprint from the download, and a preview of a build that cannot happen refuses
exactly as the build would (422, `INPUT_REQUIRED`). Both are pinned by tests.

## Verified by looking

Driven in a browser on four datasheets and the drawing compared against what the
screen itself says the part has:

```
LMP7704-SP   15 lands drawn, 14 pins   (+1: the datasheet's backside thermal pad)
DRV8825      29 lands drawn, 28 pins   (+1: the PowerPAD)
AD8628        5 lands drawn,  5 pins
RHF1201      48 lands, after answering the question
```

The count check reads the pin count off the identity block's own "Pins" row. Its
first version read `.record dd` and got whatever happened to be first, which is
not a number, so the comparison silently never ran - the same shape as the two
instruments the pre-flight had just fixed.

And then rendered and looked at: DRV8825's HTSSOP-28 comes out with fourteen
lands a side, the thermal land with its eight paste apertures and eight vias, the
body outline inside the lead rows, pin 1 marked at the top left.

Payload cost, measured over 111 parts: 8.1 KB average, 25.8 KB worst (seven
options). Negligible beside the page images already in the same response.
